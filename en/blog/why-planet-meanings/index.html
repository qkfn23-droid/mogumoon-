Why Does Each Planet Have a Meaning?

(Everything here is my personal opinion. If you disagree, your opinion is valid 'ㅅ')

Same Meanings

In astrology, each planet has a meaning.

Venus = love. Mars = war. Saturn = karma. Jupiter = luck.

The fascinating thing — civilizations far apart from each other gave the same meanings to the same planets.

Venus: Rome = Venus (goddess of love). Greece = Aphrodite (goddess of love). Vedic = Shukra (love). Babylonia = Ishtar (goddess of love).

Mars: Rome = Mars (god of war). Greece = Ares (god of war). Vedic = Mangala (warrior). Babylonia = Nergal (god of war).

Why 'ㅅ'?

Because of Color?

Scientists say Venus became "love" because it shines brightest and most beautifully in the night sky. Mars became "war" because it's red.

But isn't the Sun also fire-colored 'ㅅ' The Sun isn't war — it's life and kingship.

Both are reddish but have completely different meanings. The "because of color" explanation seems weak 'ㅅ'

And Rahu. In Vedic, Rahu = poison, illusion, obsession. But Rahu is an invisible mathematical point. It has no color. How did they assign "poison" to something invisible 'ㅅ'

Government Officials

Here's something fascinating.

Babylonia — astronomers were government officials. They reported the sky to the king.

China — the Sitianjian bureau managed astronomy and astrology.

Joseon (Korea) — the Gwansanggam existed, and the civil service exam included myeongrihak (fate studies).

Rome — there were official imperial astrologer positions.

Egypt — priests handled astronomy and astrology. It was a government role.

Civilizations far apart all treated astrology as an official government position 'ㅅ'

Egyptian Tombs

Egypt was a civilization serious about the afterlife.

Pyramids. Mummies. Book of the Dead.

And they precisely drew planetary charts on tomb ceilings.

Did they think planets were needed even after death 'ㅅ'

Stars Are All Suns

The twinkling stars in the night sky. All balls of fire like the Sun.

Up close, they look exactly like the Sun. They look like dots because they're far away.

And the twinkling isn't the stars themselves — it's caused by Earth's atmosphere. From space, stars don't twinkle, they just shine.

Twinkle twinkle stars = an illusion only visible from Earth 'ㅅ'

The Sun and Humans

Main elements of the Sun (stars): hydrogen, helium, oxygen, carbon, iron, nitrogen.

Main elements of humans: oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen, calcium, iron.

They overlap 'ㅅ'...

Scientifically, we weren't born from the Sun. A previous generation star exploded and scattered elements, and the same materials made the Sun, Earth, and humans.

The Sun isn't the parent — the Sun and humans are siblings 'ㅅ'... born from the same star.

Earth Has 1 Sun, 1 Moon

Earth has 1 Sun, 1 Moon.

But in the universe, some planets have 2-3 stars, and some have 95 satellites.

Different environments would mean completely different life forms.

Even on Earth, sunlight doesn't reach the deep sea. So deep sea creatures glow on their own, are transparent, or have no eyes.

Same Earth, but different environments make life so different.

On another planet, life could look unimaginable 'ㅅ'

It's just fascinating. Truly fascinating 'ㅅ'
